# Survey Report on Types of Blockchains and Their Real-Time Use Cases

## 1. Introduction

Blockchain is a distributed digital ledger technology used to store information securely. Data is stored in blocks, and these blocks are connected to each other using cryptographic techniques.

Blockchain allows multiple participants to share information without depending on one central authority. Once information is recorded and confirmed, changing it becomes very difficult.

Blockchain technology is used in finance, healthcare, supply chain management, education, voting systems, and many other fields.

## 2. Objectives of the Survey

1. To understand the concept of blockchain technology.
2. To study different types of blockchains.
3. To identify real-time applications of blockchain.
4. To understand the advantages and limitations of blockchain.
5. To compare different blockchain types based on their features and uses.

## 3. Types of Blockchains

There are four major types of blockchain:

### 3.1 Public Blockchain

A public blockchain is an open network where anyone can join, view transactions, and participate according to the network rules.

**Features:**
- Open to everyone.
- Decentralized network.
- Transparent transactions.
- No single organization controls the network.

**Examples:** Bitcoin and Ethereum.

**Real-time use cases:**
- Cryptocurrency transactions.
- Decentralized applications.
- Smart contracts.
- Public digital asset transfers.

### 3.2 Private Blockchain

A private blockchain is controlled by a single organization. Only authorized users can access and participate in the network.

**Features:**
- Restricted access.
- Controlled by one organization.
- Faster transaction processing in many implementations.
- Better control over data access.

**Examples:** Hyperledger Fabric and private enterprise blockchain networks.

**Real-time use cases:**
- Banking operations.
- Internal company record management.
- Supply chain tracking.
- Secure sharing of business information.

### 3.3 Consortium Blockchain

A consortium blockchain is managed by a group of organizations instead of a single organization. Only selected members can participate.

**Features:**
- Shared control among multiple organizations.
- Permission-based access.
- Suitable for business collaboration.
- Participants can share trusted information.

**Examples:** R3 Corda and consortium-based Hyperledger networks.

**Real-time use cases:**
- Banking and financial institutions.
- Supply chain management.
- Insurance claim processing.
- Sharing records between organizations.

### 3.4 Hybrid Blockchain

A hybrid blockchain combines features of public and private blockchains. Some information is kept private, while selected information can be made publicly verifiable.

**Features:**
- Combination of public and private access.
- Sensitive data can remain private.
- Selected information can be publicly verified.
- Flexible access control.

**Examples:** Dragonchain and hybrid blockchain implementations.

**Real-time use cases:**
- Healthcare record management.
- Government record systems.
- Real estate documentation.
- Business data management.

## 4. Comparison of Different Blockchain Types

| Feature | Public | Private | Consortium | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Access | Anyone | Authorized users | Selected organizations | Public and restricted |
| Control | Distributed | One organization | Multiple organizations | Mixed |
| Transparency | High | Limited | Shared among members | Selective |
| Speed | Depends on network | Often faster | Often faster | Depends on design |
| Main use | Cryptocurrency | Internal business | Organization collaboration | Private and public records |

## 5. Real-Time Applications of Blockchain

### 5.1 Banking and Finance
Blockchain helps transfer digital assets, maintain transaction records, and improve transparency in financial operations.

### 5.2 Healthcare
Blockchain can support secure sharing and verification of medical records between authorized healthcare providers.

### 5.3 Supply Chain Management
Blockchain helps track products from manufacturers to customers. It improves transparency and makes it easier to verify product history.

### 5.4 Education
Blockchain can store and verify academic certificates and qualifications. Employers and institutions can check whether certificates are genuine.

### 5.5 Voting Systems
Blockchain can be explored for maintaining transparent and verifiable digital voting records. However, secure electronic voting also requires strong identity protection, privacy, and protection against cyberattacks.

### 5.6 Real Estate
Blockchain can maintain property transaction records and support verification of ownership documents.

## 6. Advantages of Blockchain

1. **Transparency:** Transactions can be viewed by authorized participants.
2. **Security:** Cryptographic methods help protect stored information.
3. **Decentralization:** Public blockchain networks do not depend on a single central authority.
4. **Traceability:** Transactions and product histories can be tracked.
5. **Data Integrity:** Recorded information is difficult to change without detection.
6. **Efficiency:** Smart contracts can automate certain processes.

## 7. Limitations of Blockchain

1. Some blockchain networks consume significant energy.
2. Implementation and maintenance can be costly.
3. Some networks have limited transaction processing capacity.
4. Public blockchain transactions may take time to confirm.
5. Data privacy and regulatory requirements can be challenging.
6. Incorrect information entered into a blockchain may remain difficult to correct.

## 8. Findings of the Survey

From this survey, we understand that different blockchain types are designed for different requirements.

- Public blockchains are suitable for open and transparent networks.
- Private blockchains are useful for organizations requiring restricted access.
- Consortium blockchains are suitable for collaboration between multiple organizations.
- Hybrid blockchains are useful when both privacy and public verification are required.

Blockchain has applications in finance, healthcare, education, supply chains, and government services.

## 9. Conclusion

Blockchain is an important technology that provides secure, transparent, and reliable ways of storing and sharing digital information.

The survey shows that public, private, consortium, and hybrid blockchains have different features and applications. The selection of a blockchain depends on requirements such as security, privacy, transparency, speed, and access control.

Blockchain has the potential to improve many industries by providing better data management, traceability, and trust between participants.
